How to translate a YouTube video you own

  1. Export or upload the source — the original MP4, or the video file plus audio.
  2. Choose the target language and request captions, a dub, or both.
  3. Confirm the per-minute quote — before payment, valid 30 minutes.
  4. Publish — upload the SRT as a subtitle track, or add the MP3 as another audio track in YouTube Studio.

Where YouTube's native tools stop

  • Auto-translated captions are machine output too — but they are generated from YouTube's own transcript, not from yours, so names drift.
  • No dubbing — translated captions do not produce a voice track; multi-language audio must be uploaded separately.
  • Length limits — caption files have to match timing; a badly wrapped SRT gets rejected or displays awkwardly.
  • Search only indexes what exists — a video with no Portuguese caption file is invisible to Portuguese queries.

Localise the metadata too

Captions are one layer. A localised video usually needs the title, description and chapters translated as well. Keep the translated keyword in the title — it is what the viewer actually searches for — and put the original title in the description for continuity.

Frequently asked questions

Can I translate someone else's YouTube video?

Not without rights. Translation does not create a licence; re-uploading or dubbing content you do not own infringes copyright.

What file do I upload to YouTube?

Publish the SRT as a subtitle track in YouTube Studio, or use the MP3 as a multi-language audio track.

Do translated captions help search?

Yes. Caption text is indexed, so a translated SRT makes the video discoverable in that language.

Is there a free trial?

60 seconds and 100 MB, subtitles only, no account required, limited per network address.

Can I keep the original English audio?

Yes. Request captions only and the dub is skipped.
