Localization workflow that does not collapse

  1. Build a glossary first — 20–50 terms: product names, feature names, acronyms that must not be translated.
  2. Translate lesson 1 only — 60 seconds free, then a full lesson; check the glossary holds.
  3. Fix the glossary, not the lesson — correct the shared list before translating lesson 2.
  4. Batch the rest — translate the remaining lessons with a stable glossary so terminology stays consistent across the course.

Why course translation fails at lesson 7

  • Terminology drift. Lesson 1 says "dashboard", lesson 7 says "control panel" — learners notice and trust drops.
  • Reused slide text. Subtitles translate speech, not slide graphics; slides with embedded English text need separate work.
  • Instructor intros and outros. Repeated in every lesson, so an error repeats twelve times.
  • Quizzes and captions diverge. Quiz questions are written, not spoken; they need their own translation pass.

Frequently asked questions

Can I translate one lesson before the whole course?

Yes, and that is the recommended workflow: test lesson 1, fix the glossary, then batch the rest.

Do subtitles work in course platforms?

Most accept SRT (Teachable, Thinkific, Kajabi, Moodle, Udemy). Where they do not, publish the subtitled MP4.

Can I replace the lesson audio with a dub?

Yes. The MP3 can be used as a replacement narration track, though the instructor's original voice is lost.

Do you translate quizzes and slides?

No. The tool processes speech in the uploaded video; written quizzes and slide graphics need separate translation.
